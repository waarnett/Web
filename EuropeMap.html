Totals: EU 27 · Eurozone 21 · Schengen 29 · NATO 32

  1. Euro by agreement, not euro area membership. Andorra, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City use the euro as official currency and legal tender and may mint limited euro coins (never banknotes) under monetary agreements with the EU. They are outside ECB governance and outside the euro-area count of 21. (European Commission, "Legislation on the euro coins"; "The euro outside the euro area".)
  2. Andorra and San Marino: border agreements pending. Neither is a party to the Schengen acquis. EU agreements on border management with each (negotiations concluded 26 Jan 2026; initialled 25 Mar and 21 Apr 2026; Commission proposals COM(2026) 444 and COM(2026) 445 of 7 Sep 2026 on signature and provisional application) lift checks on persons at their borders with France and Spain, and with Italy, and give their residence permits Schengen-wide effect (90 days in any 180). The driver is the Entry/Exit System, fully deployed at all Schengen external borders since 10 April 2026, which would otherwise register their residents as overstayers. Provisional application begins only after the Council decisions are adopted and notifications exchanged.
  3. Monaco: treated as inside Schengen via France. Not a Schengen party, but France applies the Schengen acquis to Monaco; the France–Monaco land border is treated as internal, and France performs controls at Monaco's sea border as an external Schengen border. Monaco also forms a customs union with France.
  4. Vatican City: open border with Italy. Not a Schengen party; no systematic checks on the Rome boundary, and Italy polices St Peter's Square under the 1929 Lateran Treaty. The Holy See is the subject of international law that holds the treaties; "Vatican City" is its territory.
  5. Unilateral euro users. Montenegro and Kosovo* use the euro with no agreement of any kind with the EU; neither is in the euro area, and neither has any say in ECB policy. The ECB and Commission treat unilateral euroisation as incompatible with the Treaty path to adoption, but have not required reversal.
  6. Cyprus. An EU member legally obliged to join Schengen; the Council has not yet lifted internal border controls, and accession is not automatic — it needs a unanimous Council decision after a positive Schengen evaluation. As of October 2026 the item is not on the JHA Council agenda and Nicosia now expects full accession in 2027. Cyprus does apply parts of the acquis (SIS since 25 July 2023) and issues national, not Schengen, visas.
  7. Ireland. Opted out of Schengen by protocol, linked to the Common Travel Area with the United Kingdom; it participates selectively (e.g. the Schengen Information System). Denmark is a full Schengen member despite its justice and home affairs opt-out.
  8. Andorra and San Marino: Association Agreement. The Council authorised signature and provisional application on 16 July 2026; signature in Brussels is scheduled for 19 October 2026. It integrates both into the extended single market (with progressive access for financial services) but does not make them EU members, and Monaco's parallel negotiations remain suspended since September 2023. Andorra is already in a customs union with the EU for goods.
  9. EU candidate countries and potential candidates. Officially recognised candidate countries in this table are Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, and Türkiye (talks frozen since 2018). (Other candidates not listed in this table are Georgia, Moldova, and Ukraine.) Kosovo* applied in December 2022 and is designated a potential candidate.

NATO's official list uses Türkiye; Chinese official usage remains 土耳其 (Tǔ'ěrqí). Chinese-language equivalents for the column headings: EU 欧盟 (Ōuméng), Eurozone 欧元区 (Ōuyuánqū), Schengen Area 申根区 (Shēngēnqū), NATO 北约 (Běiyuē).
